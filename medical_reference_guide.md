# Medical Biomarker Reference Guide

## 1. Complete Blood Count (CBC)
- **Hemoglobin (Hb) & RBC Count:** Normal range 13.8-17.2 g/dL (men), 12.1-15.1 g/dL (women). Low indicates anemia. Suggestion: Eat iron-rich foods (spinach, red meat, lentils) paired with Vitamin C for absorption.
- **WBC Count & Differentials (Neutrophils, Lymphocytes, Monocytes, Eosinophils, Basophils):** Normal WBC is 4,500-11,000 cells/mcL. High indicates infection/inflammation. Low indicates immune suppression. Suggestion for low WBC: Ensure adequate Vitamin C and Zinc, avoid raw foods to prevent infection.
- **Platelet Count:** Normal 150,000-450,000 /mcL. Low increases bleeding risk. Suggestion: Consume Folate and B12 rich foods.
- **Hematocrit (HCT/PCV), MCV, MCH, MCHC, RDW:** These measure red blood cell size and volume. Abnormalities indicate specific types of anemia (e.g., high MCV = B12 deficiency anemia).

## 2. Blood Sugar
- **Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS):** Normal 70-99 mg/dL. 100-125 is pre-diabetes. >126 is diabetes.
- **HbA1c:** Normal <5.7%. 5.7%-6.4% is pre-diabetes. >6.5% is diabetes. Measures 3-month average.
- **RBS (Random) & PPBS (Postprandial):** Normal < 140 mg/dL after meals.
- **Dietary Suggestions for High Sugar:** Adopt a low-glycemic diet, avoid refined carbs, increase fiber, and exercise regularly.

## 3. Iron Studies
- **Serum Iron & Ferritin:** Ferritin measures stored iron. Low means iron deficiency. High means iron overload (hemochromatosis).
- **TIBC, UIBC, Transferrin Saturation:** Measures iron transport in the blood.
- **Suggestions for Low Iron:** Consume red meat, beans, lentils, fortified cereals. Take Vitamin C to enhance absorption.

## 4. Vitamin Tests
- **Vitamin D (25-OH):** Normal 20-50 ng/mL. Low leads to bone weakness. Suggestion: Sunlight exposure, fatty fish, fortified dairy, or D3 supplements.
- **Vitamin B12 & Folate (B9):** Normal B12 > 200 pg/mL. Low causes fatigue and nerve issues. Suggestion: Animal products, fortified nutritional yeast.
- **Vitamins A, E, K:** Fat-soluble vitamins. K is for blood clotting, A for vision, E for antioxidants.

## 5. Lipid Profile
- **Total Cholesterol:** Normal < 200 mg/dL.
- **LDL & VLDL Cholesterol:** Normal LDL < 100 mg/dL. High causes plaque buildup. Suggestion: Reduce saturated/trans fats, increase soluble fiber (oats).
- **HDL Cholesterol:** Normal > 40 mg/dL. "Good" cholesterol. Suggestion: Aerobic exercise, olive oil, nuts.
- **Triglycerides:** Normal < 150 mg/dL. Suggestion: Reduce sugar and alcohol intake.
- **Non-HDL Cholesterol:** Total minus HDL. Normal < 130 mg/dL.

## 6. Liver Function Tests (LFT)
- **SGOT (AST) & SGPT (ALT):** Normal < 50 U/L. High indicates liver stress/fatty liver. Suggestion: No alcohol, avoid processed foods, drink green tea.
- **Bilirubin (Total, Direct, Indirect):** Normal Total < 1.2 mg/dL. High causes jaundice (yellowing of skin).
- **Proteins (Albumin, Globulin, Total Protein):** Normal Albumin 3.4-5.4 g/dL. Low indicates liver/kidney disease or poor nutrition.
- **Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) & GGT:** Enzymes related to bile ducts. High GGT indicates alcohol abuse or bile issues.

## 7. Kidney Function Tests (KFT/RFT)
- **Creatinine:** Normal 0.7-1.3 mg/dL. High indicates kidney stress. Suggestion: Hydrate, avoid high doses of NSAIDs.
- **Blood Urea & BUN:** Normal BUN 7-20 mg/dL. High means dehydration or kidney issues. Suggestion: Drink water, moderate protein intake.
- **Uric Acid:** Normal 3.5-7.2 mg/dL. High causes gout. Suggestion: Avoid purine-rich foods (organ meats, shellfish, alcohol).
- **eGFR:** Normal > 90. Measures kidney filtration rate. < 60 indicates kidney disease.

## 8. Thyroid Profile
- **TSH:** Normal 0.4-4.0 mIU/L. High TSH = Hypothyroidism (underactive). Low TSH = Hyperthyroidism (overactive).
- **Free T3, Free T4, Total T3, Total T4:** Thyroid hormones.
- **Suggestions for Hypothyroidism:** Consume iodine (iodized salt, seaweed), selenium (Brazil nuts), and avoid large amounts of raw goitrogens (cabbage, broccoli).

## 9. Electrolytes & Minerals
- **Sodium, Chloride:** Regulates water balance.
- **Potassium:** Normal 3.5-5.0 mEq/L. Vital for heart function. Suggestion for low: Bananas, potatoes, spinach.
- **Calcium, Magnesium, Phosphorus:** Bone health and nerve function. Suggestion for low Calcium: Dairy, leafy greens, fortified plant milks.

## 10. Inflammation & Infection Markers
- **ESR, CRP, hs-CRP:** Normal CRP < 3.0 mg/L. High indicates inflammation. Suggestion: Anti-inflammatory diet (Mediterranean diet, Omega-3s, turmeric).
- **Procalcitonin:** Marker for severe bacterial infection.

## 11. Hormone Tests
- **Testosterone, Estrogen, Progesterone, LH, FSH:** Reproductive hormones.
- **Prolactin:** Milk production hormone.
- **Cortisol:** Stress hormone. High levels suggest chronic stress. Suggestion: Sleep hygiene, meditation, adaptogens.
- **Insulin:** Regulates blood sugar. High fasting insulin indicates insulin resistance.

## 12. Heart-Related Markers & Others
- **Troponin, CK-MB, BNP:** Markers for heart attack and heart failure. Immediate emergency medical attention required if elevated.
- **Blood Group:** A, B, AB, O.
- **Rheumatoid Factor (RF) & ANA:** Autoimmune markers.
- **D-Dimer:** Indicates blood clots.
- **Homocysteine:** High levels risk heart disease. Suggestion: B-vitamins (Folate, B6, B12).
- **Zinc & Copper:** Trace minerals. Zinc is crucial for immunity.
